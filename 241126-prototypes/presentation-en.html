How to judge them

Three questions, not “which looks nicest”

01

Who is it for?

Operators fixing the plant, or managers checking results? Each direction picks one.

02

What comes first?

Live state (A), meaning (B), place (C), or familiarity (D).

03

What does it cost?

Build effort now, and how hard it is to maintain as sites and buildings grow.

Score each direction against these while we go through them. We’ll vote at the end.

  • Ask people to write down one score from 1 to 5 per direction as we go, so the vote isn’t only about whichever one we saw last.
Ground rules

What every direction keeps

Same data, same screens

Sign-in, Dashboard, Energy and Alerts, all built on one shared mock dataset. Only the experience changes.

Energy distribution stays

The sources → uses widget people like is in all four, restyled to fit each one.

Real terms and units

Heat pump, geo drills, AHU, DHW; kWh, kW, €/MWh, COP, °C, ppm. Same alert triage ideas as today.

Works on a phone

Every screen works down to 375 px, some better than others.

Light and dark

Where it fits the direction. A is dark only on purpose.

Four data states

Loading, empty, error and offline on every dashboard, so we can compare the bad days too.

  • This keeps the discussion on UX, not on content: if a number looks wrong, it’s wrong in all four.
  • The sign-in screens are only illustrations. We are not choosing a sign-in method today.
A

Control Room

For operators and technicians
A dark, dense console where everything is live and you can act from the keyboard.

Pros

  • Fastest for daily operation: everything that’s live fits on one screen
  • ⌘K palette and keyboard triage (J/K, A, M, R) keep working with hundreds of alerts and devices
  • Fits a plant-room or control-room wall screen

Cons

  • Overwhelming for managers, finance and occupants
  • Dark and dense, so it’s the weakest on a phone
  • Tiles you can rearrange are another layout to store and support
Pick this ifmost people using it spend their day running the plant.
Build effort
  • Demo: press ⌘K in the frame, then go to Alerts and use J/K and A.
  • The ticker and the flashing “Peak load” number are what make it feel live.
  • Ask: “Would our facility manager open this every morning?”
B

Nordic Calm

For managers, finance and occupants
Light and editorial: it tells you what the numbers mean before showing them.

Pros

  • Anyone gets the point in 10 seconds: “energy is 8% below last Tuesday”
  • Best on a phone, and the closest to the reports funders and IPCEI ask for
  • Alerts grouped by what to do, which means fewer alarms people learn to ignore

Cons

  • The sentences need trustworthy data and copy rules, in LV and EN. That is the hidden cost
  • Too shallow for operators: exact values take extra clicks
  • Roomy layout means less on screen at a glance
Pick this ifthe people we most need to convince are managers, funders and occupants.
Build effort
  • Demo: open Energy, the five-chapter report with recommendations at the end.
  • The layout itself is low effort. The real work is generating sentences that are always correct.
  • Ask: “Who would read this briefing every morning?”
C

Spatial

For mixed users, demos and the TV display
The campus map is the navigation: you find problems by where they are.

Pros

  • Shows where straight away, which suits a campus with distinct buildings
  • The most memorable for demos, visitors and IPCEI showcases
  • The time scrubber makes daily patterns easy to see

Cons

  • Highest cost: every site and new building needs map geometry, kept up to date
  • Exact numbers and tables are hidden behind clicks
  • Harder to make accessible, and it can slow down with many rooms
Pick this ifshowing the pilot matters as much as running it.
Build effort
  • Demo: click a building, drag the time slider, then switch the colouring to Comfort.
  • Our real app already has a 3D facility page in beta, so C is partly a direction we have started on.
  • Ask: “Is this the main app, or the TV and 3D view?”
D

Refined

For the same users as today
Today’s app with the same structure, polished: one card style, clearer numbers, dark mode.

Pros

  • Lowest risk: no retraining, and it can ship bit by bit
  • Keeps everything people already like, the Energy distribution widget included
  • Colour and font presets let us adjust the brand without another redesign

Cons

  • Least new value: same structure, so today’s structural problems stay
  • Explains less than B and feels less live than A
  • Risk that people see it as “nicer, but nothing changed”
Pick this iftoday’s structure works and we want visible improvement soon.
Build effort
  • Demo: the swatch button in the top bar switches between the four colour and font presets.
  • Most of D is CSS tokens and shared components, so it can land alongside everything else.
