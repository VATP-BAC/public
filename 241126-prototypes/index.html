Four directions for the VATP app

A, B and C each try a different UX: how you sign in, how you navigate, and what the first screen tells you. D is today’s app with a face-lift, as a low-risk baseline. All four use the same mock data, so compare how the experience feels and works rather than the numbers. Each has four screens: login, dashboard, energy and alerts.

▶ Present to the team (LV) English deck 9 slides with live screens, pros and cons, a comparison and a vote

A

Control Room

For operators and technicians. A dense, dark, always-live console where every number is one keystroke away.

Inter Tight for UI · JetBrains Mono for numbers
  • Icon rail, ⌘K command palette, live ticker
  • Tiles you can drag to rearrange
  • Keyboard triage: J/K to move, A to ack, M to assign, R to resolve

B

Nordic Calm

For managers, finance and occupants. An editorial, light layout that explains what happened in plain sentences before it shows any charts.

Fraunces for headlines · Inter for text
  • Top nav with no sidebar, and a daily “Today” briefing
  • Energy as a five-chapter story with recommendations
  • Alerts sorted into Needs you, Watching and Resolved
Start at login →TodayEnergyAlertsLogin
C

Spatial Campus

For everyone, and for demos and visitors. The campus map is the navigation: click a building, then a floor, then a room. The interface floats on glass panels above the map.

Manrope for UI · IBM Plex Mono for data
  • Isometric campus coloured by energy, comfort or CO₂
  • Time scrubber at the bottom to replay the day
  • Alerts appear as pins, and clicking one flies the map to it
Start at login →CampusEnergyAlertsLogin
D

Today’s app, refined

Lowest risk, for the same users as today. It keeps the current brand, navigation and pages, but tightens and polishes everything.

DM Serif Display for headings · Space Grotesk for UI, plus Geist Mono for numbers
  • Same sidebar groups, building switcher, breadcrumbs and TV link
  • Every card uses one radius, one shadow and one header style; KPIs get large serif numbers
  • Dashboard keeps its widgets: Needs attention, forecast with cheap and expensive price bands, distribution, plant, price outlook
  • Energy is split into tabs; alerts become an inbox with a detail pane; dark mode is included
  • A swatch button in the top bar switches between 4 colour and font presets: Classic, Baltic Coast, Pine & Copper and Graphite

Questions for the team

  1. Who opens the app most often: operators fixing things, or managers checking results?
  2. Should the first screen show live state (A), meaning (B) or place (C)?
  3. Is a face-lift (D) enough, or is it worth a bigger change?
  4. When the plant gateway drops (a real risk on site), who needs to know, and how loudly? Compare the Offline state across A–D.

These are static prototypes, not connected to real data. On any screen, the floating pill at the bottom switches direction on the same screen (press H to hide it).
